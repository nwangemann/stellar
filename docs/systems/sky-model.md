# Sky model

`src/core/catalog/sky.ts` turns the catalog, a location and a time into altitude and azimuth. `src/core/astro/horizontal.ts` applies precession and sidereal time; it ignores refraction, nutation and proper motion. `useMoment` supplies the place and time, refreshes every minute and falls back to 40° N, 105° W, labelled "Demo location". `useSky` and `useMoment` take an offset in milliseconds to show another time, which the [time panel](ui.md) sets. `useSky` also gives `realSun`, the Sun's true direction whatever time is shown.

A refresh must be cheap, so the UI thread never gets a whole sky. `MARKS` is what can be drawn, the same at every moment: stars as J2000 vectors and the ids of the bodies. It is copied once. Each refresh sends a `SkyTurn` through a shared value: the rotation from J2000 to the horizon, the directions of the bodies and the Moon's phase. `createSky` uses the same rotation, for the JS thread only. Never capture its objects in a worklet. The canvas, HUD and looks are memoized so that a refresh does not render them; rebuilding their worklets is the slow part. Measured on 2026-11-24 in a development build on the simulator: a refresh takes about 16 ms of JS, down from about 190 ms. Release builds, phones and slider drags are unmeasured.

`createSky` adds the Sun, Moon and naked-eye planets as objects of kind `body`. They can be locked on to in Stars (and in the hidden Layers mode), within each mode's magnitude limit; Constellations draws them but ignores them otherwise. For their details, see [bodies](bodies.md). `BodyMarks` draws them; see [UI](ui.md). `src/core/astro/phase.ts` gives the Moon's lit fraction and the direction of its bright limb.

`src/core/astro/bodies.ts` gives the topocentric place of the Sun, Moon and naked-eye planets through `astronomy-engine` (ADR 15). These are coordinates of date, so pass them to `toAltAz` without `precess`. Tests check them against JPL Horizons values in `src/core/astro/fixtures`.

`src/core/catalog/search.ts` finds bodies, constellations and drawn stars by name. Case, accents and superscripts do not matter, and "α CMa", "alpha cma" and "Alpha Canis Majoris" are the same; `genitives.ts` holds the Latin forms. Whole-name matches come first, then names that start with the text, then later words. Ties go to bodies, constellations, then brighter stars. The index is built on the first search. `aimAt` in `aim.ts` gives the object to point at to find one; see [Find in sky](find.md).
